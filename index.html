Complete Guide to UAE End of Service Gratuity & Labor Law Regulations

Understanding UAE End of Service Benefits (EOSB)

The United Arab Emirates (UAE) has established a robust legal framework to protect the financial rights of employees working in the private and public sectors. One of the fundamental pillars of this framework is the End of Service Benefit (EOSB), commonly referred to as gratuity. Gratuity serves as a monetary compensation provided by an employer to an employee upon the termination or resignation from their employment contract, provided specific conditions under UAE Labor Law are met.

Navigating the rules surrounding gratuity can be complex due to factors such as contract types, length of service, basic salary calculations, and recent legislative updates. Key Eligibility Criteria for UAE Gratuity

To qualify for end-of-service gratuity in the UAE, an employee must satisfy primary legal provisions outlined in Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 regarding the Regulation of Employment Relationships. The critical requirements include:

  • Minimum Service Period: An employee must complete at least one full continuous year of service with the employer to be eligible for gratuity payout. Continuous service interrupted by unpaid leave without pay does not count towards total service years.
  • Contract Type: Following recent labor law reforms, all private sector contracts in the UAE have been standardized into limited (fixed-term) contracts. Unlimited contracts have been phased out.
  • Lawful Termination or Resignation: Gratuity is applicable whether the employee resigns or is terminated by the employer, provided the termination does not fall under Article 44 (gross misconduct).

How Gratuity Payout is Calculated

The calculation of end-of-service gratuity is strictly based on the employee's **basic salary**. Allowances such as housing, transportation, utility, or performance bonuses are excluded from the basic figure used for calculations.

Standard Calculation Formula:

  • 1 to 5 Years of Continuous Service: The employee is entitled to 21 days' basic salary for each completed year of service.
  • More than 5 Years of Continuous Service: The employee is entitled to 30 days' basic salary for each additional year after the fifth year.
  • Fractional Years: If an employee works beyond one year, the additional months and days are calculated on a pro-rata basis.

Furthermore, UAE Labor Law caps total gratuity benefits: the total payout cannot exceed two years' worth of total basic salary, regardless of how many decades the employee has worked for the company.

Impact of Contract Termination & Resignation

Under old regulations, resigning under an unlimited contract often led to reductions in gratuity depending on whether the service was between 1 to 3 years or 3 to 5 years. However, under Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021, these reductions have been largely eliminated for standardized contracts.

When an employee completes one year, they earn their full 21 days' basic salary per year upon resignation or termination. Knowing these precise figures eliminates misunderstandings between employers and departing personnel. Important Deductions from Gratuity Settlement

While employees expect a clean payout upon departure, employers are permitted by law to make certain lawful deductions from the final gratuity total under specific circumstances:

  1. Unpaid Loans and Advances: Any outstanding personal debts, salary advances, or company loans extended to the employee.
  2. Company Property Damage: Cost repairs or losses incurred due to gross negligence or willful destruction of company assets.
  3. Unreturned Assets: Failure to return company equipment such as laptops, phones, access cards, or vehicles.

MOHRE Regulations and Compliance

The Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE) enforces strict oversight on final settlement payouts. Employers are required by law to settle all end-of-service dues, including accrued leave pay and final month salary, within **14 days** from the contract termination date.

If an employer fails or refuses to pay within this timeframe, the worker has the full legal right to register an official labor complaint via MOHRE digital portals or call centers. Delaying final settlements without valid legal cause exposes companies to administrative fines and restrictions on issuing new work permits.

Voluntary Alternative Savings Schemes in the UAE

In recent years, the UAE Government introduced voluntary alternative end-of-service benefits schemes (such as the Workplace Savings Scheme in Dubai and national investment funds). Under these voluntary programs, employers invest monthly contributions into regulated investment funds on behalf of their employees.

Upon departure, employees receive their base principal investment along with returns earned through low-risk or capital-guaranteed investment options. This modern approach aims to enhance financial security for expats and modernise traditional severance systems.
